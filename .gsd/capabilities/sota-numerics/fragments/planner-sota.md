SOTA-numerics research discipline for planning is advisory here, but a blocking plan:post gate mechanically enforces it and will halt planning if this plan is not compliant.
For every non-trivial mechanism or library choice, name at least 2 current alternatives, each carrying an attached source — a URL or a backticked doc-ref — with a discoverable date, and state which ranked criterion decided the pick, in this order: performance, then simplicity and lines of code, then ecosystem support, then maintenance overhead.
Write the section as a `## Alternatives Considered` heading, followed by bold-named mechanism alternatives as either bullets (`- **Name**: ...`) or framed Markdown table rows, each carrying a URL or backticked doc-ref citation plus a 4-digit year, and a `Decided by:` line naming one of performance, simplicity, LOC, ecosystem, or maintenance.
Project-local reasoning may appear under the exact third-level heading `### Internal design alternatives`, using the same bold-named bullet or framed-table shapes, until a peer level-three heading, including a bare `###`, ends that scope. Internal entries need no external citation or date, do not count toward the two mechanism alternatives, and cannot lend evidence to a mechanism entry. Mechanism entries outside that exact subsection retain every citation, date, count, and `Decided by:` requirement.
A plan that is pure config, documentation, or a mechanical rename with no real mechanism choice may write `N/A — no mechanism choice` as the entire section body instead of fabricating a comparison — do not invent alternatives to satisfy the gate when none genuinely exist.
A phase that already has a SPEC.md still requires this section in PLAN.md: SPEC.md locks what is built, this section documents how the mechanism was chosen, and the gate checks PLAN.md, not SPEC.md.
Placeholder citations are rejected mechanically: URLs whose exact bare authority is example.com, example.org, example.net, or localhost, plus bare TODO or TBD doc-refs, fail the gate — cite something real.
The gate requires at least one cited year per alternative to fall inside the six-year window, not every year; a canonical work's old year (Kahan summation, IEEE 754, a classic BLAS paper) is neither rejected nor counted toward that window on its own, so pairing the canonical source with a current doc, release note, or benchmark that supplies an in-window date is the correct, well-researched move, not a workaround for a gate quirk.
Two alternatives is the floor, not the target: list a third when the choice is genuinely close, but do not pad the section with alternatives that are obviously worse just to satisfy the count.
A pick justified by performance alone needs no further defense; a pick that trades performance away must show which lower-ranked criterion won and why, in the same order the gate expects.
A plan goes with the grain when the code it asks for matches the conventions already in the project — its naming, its error shape, its module layout, its test placement — so a reader cannot tell from the code which file is new.
Every task in a plan names a bound the executor can check, so "done" is decidable without asking the author.
